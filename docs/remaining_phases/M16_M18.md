# M16–M18 — BGC Visual Fidelity Expansion

## Mission

Transform the functional interactive BGC experience into a visually convincing, grounded city experience.

Previous milestones have already proven:

```text
real BGC geography
6,982 LOD2 buildings
94 spatial tiles
7 grounded LOD1 buildings
dynamic tile runtime
LOD switching
first-person walking
collision
building selection
search
guided tour
grounded street environment
```

M15 foreground runtime validation has been manually confirmed.

Treat:

```text
M12: PASS
M13: PASS
M14: PASS
M15: PASS
```

The new goal is NOT to increase detail everywhere.

The goal is:

> Spend visual-detail budget only where users will notice it, while improving the shared visual quality of the entire district.

This combined phase contains three internal gates:

```text
M16
SELECT AND PREPARE IMPORTANT BUILDINGS

        ↓ GATE

M17
RECONSTRUCT PRIORITY BUILDINGS

        ↓ GATE

M18
POLISH THE SHARED CITY ENVIRONMENT
```

Primary model:

SOL

Astra:

NO by default.

Use Astra only for explicitly bounded visual reconstruction failures defined later.

---

# M16 — Selective Building Upgrade Planning

## 1. Do not research all 6,982 buildings

This is critical.

Most buildings should remain:

```text
LOD2
```

for the foreseeable future.

Detailed reconstruction should concentrate on buildings that materially improve the walking/tour experience.

---

## 2. Preserve the current seven LOD1 assets

Existing approved detailed buildings remain regression assets.

Do not unnecessarily remodel:

```text
Central Square
W Global Center
One Maridien
JY Campos Centre
Verve Residences Tower 2
UNIQLO
C1
```

They may receive shared-material improvements in M18, but their validated geometry should not be casually changed.

---

## 3. Build a priority candidate pool

Start from the 6,982 canonical entities.

Prioritize buildings near:

```text
guided-tour route
High Street
major pedestrian corridors
existing detailed buildings
important plazas
major intersections
high-visibility skyline locations
```

Use existing geographic/entity information first.

Do not begin web research yet.

---

## 4. Rank candidates using multiple dimensions

For each candidate assess:

```text
LANDMARK_IMPORTANCE
PEDESTRIAN_VISIBILITY
TOUR_VISIBILITY
ARCHITECTURAL_DISTINCTIVENESS
CURRENT_LOD2_VISUAL_ERROR
IDENTITY_CONFIDENCE
FOOTPRINT_CONFIDENCE
HEIGHT_CONFIDENCE
PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_AVAILABILITY
RECONSTRUCTION_COMPLEXITY
```

Keep these dimensions separately visible.

A total score may be used for ranking, but do not hide reasoning behind one number.

---

## 5. Define upgrade tiers

Classify buildings:

### Tier A — Landmark

Important enough that users are expected to notice it specifically.

Target:

```text
LOD1
possibly future LOD0
```

### Tier B — Important context

Strongly visible from primary pedestrian/tour areas.

Target:

```text
LOD1 when evidence supports it
```

### Tier C — Background

Normal urban massing.

Remain:

```text
LOD2
```

Most BGC buildings should remain Tier C.

---

## 6. Prefer experiential importance over fame

A globally recognizable building outside the walking experience may matter less than a smaller building that dominates High Street.

Consider:

```text
how often users see it
how close users get
whether it defines a street/corner
whether it serves as navigation landmark
```

---

## 7. Identify geometry errors with highest perceptual cost

Some LOD2 buildings may have incorrect fallback height but low visual importance.

Others may strongly damage skyline/street appearance.

Rank height corrections according to:

```text
visual prominence
+
height uncertainty
+
pedestrian/tour relevance
```

Do NOT attempt to correct the 94%+ procedural-height population wholesale.

---

## 8. Select a bounded reconstruction batch

Target approximately:

```text
8–12 new LOD1 buildings
```

for M17.

Use fewer if evidence quality is poor.

Do not select 20+ buildings simply to increase asset count.

Include multiple architectural archetypes where practical:

```text
tower
podium tower
office
retail
irregular footprint
mixed-use
```

---

## 9. Evidence gate

Only selected candidates enter evidence research.

For every selected target:

```text
identity
height
massing
major facades
roof
materials
entrances
```

must be assessed.

Use existing evidence tooling.

Classify:

```text
READY
READY_WITH_GAPS
NOT_READY
```

Only READY / READY_WITH_GAPS proceed.

Replace NOT_READY targets rather than spending unlimited research effort.

---

## 10. Rights discipline remains unchanged

Separate:

```text
REUSABLE
RESEARCH_ONLY
UNKNOWN
```

Public images do NOT automatically become reusable project assets.

Use research-only photographs for observations where allowed by the established rights policy.

Do not embed them as textures.

---

Generate:

```text
docs/M16_PRIORITY_BUILDINGS.md

data/reports/
    m16-priority-ranking.json
    m16-selected-buildings.json
```

For each selected building record:

```text

```

M16 passes when:

# M17 — Priority Landmark Reconstruction

## 12. Reuse the proven reconstruction system

Do NOT design another building pipeline.

Use:

```text
evidence package
      ↓
reconstruction_spec
      ↓
shared Blender framework
      ↓
QA cameras
      ↓
reference comparison
      ↓
GLB
      ↓
registry
      ↓
runtime
```

Schema remains:

```text
1.0
```

unless a real architectural requirement cannot be represented.

---

## 13. Reconstruct in waves

Do not reconstruct all targets simultaneously.

Use:

```text
Wave A
3–4 buildings

        ↓ regression gate

Wave B
remaining targets
```

This prevents repeating a new framework defect across the entire batch.

---

## 14. LOD1 remains medium fidelity

Target:

```text
recognizable massing
major facade rhythm
important recesses
important entrances
major materials
roof silhouette
street relationship
```

Do NOT model:

```text
tiny mullion details
door handles
HVAC piping
interior furniture
small decorative trim
individual retail contents
```

unless uniquely important.

---

## 15. Massing first

For every target:

```text
footprint
height
major volumes
setbacks
roof
```

must pass QA before facade detail.

Do not decorate incorrect massing.

---

## 16. Strongest facade first

Implement the best-supported facade/corner first.

Then proceed toward weaker evidence.

Use:

```text
strong evidence
→ higher specificity

weak evidence
→ conservative geometry
```

Do not make every side equally detailed.

---

## 17. Preserve evidence states

Every major component remains:

```text
VERIFIED
INFERRED
ESTIMATED
PROCEDURAL
UNKNOWN
CONFLICTED
```

Do not replace epistemic status with visual confidence.

---

## 18. Correct high-value heights during reconstruction

For selected buildings only, research better height evidence where it materially affects reconstruction.

A verified or strong estimated height on an important landmark is useful.

Do NOT expand this into city-wide height research.

---

## 19. Reuse existing shared primitives

Track:

```text
existing shared primitives
new shared primitives
target-specific configuration
target-specific code
```

Prefer configuration.

Only add a new shared primitive if it represents a genuinely reusable architectural concept.

---

## 20. Continue authoring/runtime separation

Blender:

```text
semantic/editable components
```

Runtime:

```text
optimized GLB structure
```

Merge runtime geometry only where it reduces cost without destroying required metadata.

---

## 21. Maintain compact semantic metadata

Preserve:

```text
entity_id
component_id
component_type
evidence_state
LOD
```

Canonical research evidence remains outside the GLB.

Blender's glTF exporter supports custom properties as glTF extras, so retain the existing metadata approach.

---

## 22. Materials during M17

Use the current material library.

Do not solve M18's entire material system separately for each building.

Assign broad families such as:

```text
glass
warm stone
concrete
metal
painted panel
storefront
```

M18 will harmonize their presentation.

---

## 23. Visual QA

Use approximately:

```text
2–3 refinement passes
```

per major target.

Fix in this order:

```text
placement
massing
silhouette
large facade regions
entrances
materials
minor details
```

Do not endlessly refine uncertain details.

---

## 24. Astra escalation

Astra is justified only when:

1. evidence coverage is GOOD;
2. geometry is structurally valid;
3. Sol has performed multiple focused refinements;
4. a significant visual mismatch remains;
5. the mismatch is caused by difficult spatial interpretation rather than missing evidence.

Example valid Astra task:

```text
Correct the geometry of this specific landmark podium
using these four reference views while preserving
validated footprint, height and other facades.
```

Never give Astra:

```text
make BGC realistic
```

or:

```text
reconstruct all landmarks
```

---

## 25. LOD0 policy

Do NOT automatically create LOD0.

During M17 identify whether any landmark genuinely needs it.

Possible candidates must satisfy:

```text
very high experiential importance
+
users approach very closely
+
LOD1 visibly insufficient
+
strong evidence exists
```

It is acceptable for:

```text
LOD0 count = 0
```

after M17.

---

```text
LOD2 → LOD1 → LOD2
```

At minimum:


Do not rerender every building unnecessarily when only target-specific files changed.

# M18 — Shared City Visual Polish

## 29. M18 objective

Improve the visual language of the entire experience without rebuilding the city.

Focus on systems that provide large visual benefit per unit of runtime cost.

Primary areas:

```text
materials
lighting
ground
roads
sidewalks
vegetation
street environment
atmosphere
visual consistency
```

# MATERIALS

## 30. Establish a compact PBR material library

Use `MeshStandardMaterial` / glTF metal-rough PBR principles.

Three.js's standard material uses metallic/roughness physically based shading and benefits from suitable environment lighting.

Create reusable families approximately:

```text
BGC_GLASS_DARK
BGC_GLASS_LIGHT
BGC_CONCRETE_LIGHT
BGC_CONCRETE_DARK
BGC_STONE_WARM
BGC_METAL_DARK
BGC_METAL_LIGHT
BGC_ASPHALT
BGC_SIDEWALK
BGC_GRASS
BGC_SOIL
```

Do not create hundreds of materials.

---

## 31. Match Blender and web material conventions

Blender's glTF exporter supports metal/rough PBR channels including:

```text
Base Color
Metallic
Roughness
AO
Normal
Emissive
```

when using supported Principled BSDF structures.

Keep authored landmark materials compatible with runtime materials.

---

## 32. Avoid fake photorealism

Target:

```text
stylized-realistic
clean
architecturally readable
coherent
```

not:

```text
photographic digital twin
```

The project contains large amounts of uncertain geometry.

Highly photorealistic materials on approximate buildings can increase perceived inaccuracy.

---

# TEXTURES

## 33. Introduce textures only where they provide high return

Good candidates:

```text
asphalt
sidewalk
grass
generic concrete
generic stone
subtle normal variation
```

Avoid unique photographic building textures.

---

## 34. Texture reuse

Prefer a tiny reusable texture library.

Do not package identical asphalt/grass/concrete textures in every GLB.

Use shared runtime assets where architecture allows.

---

## 35. Measure before using KTX2

Track:

```text
texture bytes
GPU texture memory proxy
load time
network transfer
```

If textures become significant:

evaluate KTX2/Basis.

Three.js `KTX2Loader` supports GPU-compressed KTX2/Basis textures and selects a supported GPU format after renderer capability detection.

Do NOT add KTX2 merely because it is available.

---

# LIGHTING

## 36. Redesign lighting as a scene-level system

The existing lighting should become intentional.

Start with:

```text
sky/ambient contribution
+
primary directional sun
+
environment map if beneficial
```

Avoid many dynamic lights.

---

## 37. PBR environment lighting

Evaluate a lightweight environment map for reflections/ambient response.

This can materially improve:

```text
glass
metal
stone
```

without adding architectural geometry.

Measure performance and asset cost.

---

## 38. Shadows

Use shadows selectively.

Three.js shadow maps effectively rerender shadow-casting objects from each shadow-casting light, so city-wide unrestricted shadows can become expensive.

Prefer:

```text
one shadow-casting directional light
```

if shadows are enabled.

---

## 39. Limit the shadow region

Do NOT shadow-map the entire 4.7 km² BGC area at high resolution.

Use an approach appropriate to the active pedestrian region.

Potential strategy:

```text
shadow camera follows local walking region
```

or equivalent.

Measure before/after performance.

---

## 40. Shadow quality settings

Provide simple quality behavior if needed:

```text
LOW
FULL
```

or integrate into existing environment quality.

Do not create a large graphics settings system yet.

---

# ROADS AND GROUND

## 41. Improve road readability

Upgrade major roads from simple geographic surfaces using:

```text
better asphalt material
slight tonal variation
important crossings where grounded
```

Do not manually paint the entire road network.

---

## 42. Sidewalk/pedestrian distinction

Visually differentiate:

```text
vehicle roads
pedestrian corridors
plazas
sidewalk-like surfaces
parks
```

High Street should read immediately as a pedestrian-oriented axis.

---

## 43. Do not invent lane-level accuracy

Unless road markings are geographically/source supported:

avoid detailed lane lines, arrows and curb rules.

M18 is visual improvement, not road-survey reconstruction.

---

# VEGETATION

## 44. Improve tree appearance

Current mapped trees remain geographically grounded.

Keep positions.

Improve the shared tree models/materials rather than adding arbitrary new trees.

Potentially create a small deterministic variation set:

```text
TREE_A
TREE_B
TREE_C
```

---

## 45. Preserve instancing

Trees, lamps, benches, bollards and similar repeated props must remain instanced.

Do not trade visual polish for thousands of draw calls.

---

## 46. Deterministic instance variation

Variation may include:

```text
scale
rotation
one of a small set of models
```

using deterministic seeded values.

Do not randomly change appearance on each page load.

---

# ATMOSPHERE

## 47. Add subtle distance treatment

Evaluate:

```text
fog / atmospheric distance fade
```

to improve city-scale depth and reduce harsh distant geometry.

Keep it subtle.

Do not obscure navigation landmarks.

---

## 48. Sky/background

Replace purely technical-looking background color if an inexpensive environment treatment improves presentation.

Keep visual styling consistent with the project's grounded purpose.

Avoid a dramatic fantasy sky.

---

Ensure shared:

```text

tone mapping
material response
ground relationship
```

creates visual coherence.
